I build language models that think less and still answer well, and I study what breaks inside them when they do.

I am a Ph.D. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Southern California. I work in the SPORT Lab with Prof. Massoud Pedram. My research covers post-training and alignment, test-time sampling for reasoning, KV cache reduction, activation steering, and reinforcement learning for large models. I care about methods that hold up under an honest baseline and that run on real hardware.

In summer 2026 I was an Applied Research Intern on Apple's SIML team in Seattle, where I traced response collapse in instruction-tuned models back to reward hacking under rubric-based RL. I also work with Intel Labs on long-context methods for Transformer and Mamba models.

If you want to talk about efficient inference, post-training, or interpretability, send me an email.

Research

Large models spend most of their cost at inference, and most of that cost buys tokens nobody reads. Three questions organise my work.

How much of test-time search can be moved into the model? Sampling many chains and reranking them is expensive. I study sequence-level samplers and distillation targets that get similar quality from one pass.

What does a model actually keep while reasoning? Chain-of-thought traces and KV caches are both far larger than the information they carry. I work on steering, eviction, and compression methods that cut them without retraining.

Why does alignment break under small edits? A short added constraint can turn a helpful model into a refusing one. I trace these failures to the post-training stage that causes them, and I look for fixes upstream rather than patches at the output.
